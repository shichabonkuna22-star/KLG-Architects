History

From Glennie and Egan to KLG.

KLG Architects grew out of the Köper Levin George practice. The line begins with Glennie McIntosh and John Egan in Cape Town in the 1930s, joined by Henk Sikkel in the 1940s — then named Glennie, Egan and Sikkel.

The work now ranges from houses to hospitality, education, institutions, and master plans. The studio keeps an affinity for vernacular building traditions, with a taste for simplicity in a contemporary form. The practice is led by directors Geoff George, Jan Douglas, and Tyrone Bloch.

Site first

A house in Oranjezicht and a cabin in Kogelberg are read from the ground: slope, wind, fynbos, and the existing room. The drawing follows that reading.

02

Hand and conversation

Work is made in conversation with clients, builders, and the last generation of the plan. Details are few, and they are meant to be built.

03

Honest materials

Pine, limestone, reused brick, timber in the reserve, copper that greens. Finishes are withheld where the structure can speak.
